Privacy Policy

Durak · Last updated October 2026

Durak does not ask who you are. There is no account to create, and your game stays on your iPhone. A few services work alongside the app: RevenueCat, which handles purchases; an ad provider, which shows ads to players who have not bought Premium; and Google Firebase and Mixpanel, which give us anonymous usage statistics and crash reports. This page explains what each of them receives.

Your game stays on your device

Your progress on each line, the levels and daily puzzles you have finished, your streak, your coin balance and your settings (game language, sound, haptics) are stored in the app's own storage on your iPhone. The word lists are built into the app, and the game works without an internet connection. We operate no servers that hold this information and have no way to see it.

If you back up your iPhone to iCloud or to a computer, this data is included in that backup like any other app's data. That backup is handled by Apple or by your computer, not by us.

Purchases

Durak offers coin packs and an auto-renewable Premium subscription. Payment is handled by Apple through the App Store; we never see or receive your payment details.

To deliver purchases and recognise a subscription across your devices, the app uses RevenueCat. RevenueCat receives an anonymous identifier generated on your device, along with your purchase history, basic technical information such as device model, operating system version and country, and whether the subscription screen was shown and closed. This identifier is not linked to your name, email address or Apple ID, and nothing about your game is sent with it.

Apple's handling of App Store transactions is covered by Apple's Privacy Policy.

Advertising

If you have not bought Premium, Durak shows ads: sometimes a full-screen ad when you open the app, a full-screen ad between some levels (never during your first five levels, and not after every level), and videos you can choose to watch for coins, including a short offer that appears when you are stuck on a puzzle. Each installation of Durak is randomly assigned one ad provider, either Google AdMob or Unity LevelPlay (ironSource and Unity Ads), and keeps it.

When an ad is requested, that provider receives technical information it needs to deliver and measure the ad: your IP address (from which an approximate location can be derived), device and operating system information such as model, language and connection type, the app's identifier and how you interact with the ad. Google uses this as described in Google's Privacy Policy, and Unity as described in Unity's Privacy Policy.

  • Consent in the EEA, UK and Switzerland. Before any ad is requested there, Durak shows a consent form provided by Google, whichever ad provider your installation uses, and ads are personalised only if you agree. You can change your choice at any time in Durak's Settings under "Ad privacy choices".
  • Tracking permission. After that, iOS asks whether Durak may track you across other companies' apps and websites. If you allow it, the ad provider may use your device's advertising identifier to show more relevant ads. If you do not, the advertising identifier is not shared and ads are still shown, just less tailored. You can change this at any time in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking.
  • Premium. Premium players see no ads, and Durak does not request ads, ask for consent or ask for tracking permission for them.

Durak itself does not collect, store or sell any of this information. Ads never receive your progress, your words or your purchases.

Usage analytics and crash reports

To see how the game is played (for example, where players stop, whether they come back, and how ads and purchases affect that) and to fix crashes, Durak sends a set of usage events to Google Firebase (Analytics and Crashlytics) and Mixpanel. The events cover: starting, finishing or leaving a level or the daily puzzle; using a hint; earning and spending coins; ads being shown and rewards earned; the Premium and coin screens being opened; and purchases being completed. They also carry a few settings that describe how you play: game language, whether you have Premium, which ad provider your installation uses, roughly how many levels you have finished, your tracking-permission choice and whether the app came from TestFlight or the App Store. They carry no name, email address, words you typed or payment details.

Along with these events, Firebase and Mixpanel receive a random identifier created for this installation of the app, and basic device and app information (such as device model, operating system version, app version and language). Firebase derives an approximate location (country or region) from your IP address; Mixpanel is configured not to use your IP address for location. If the app crashes, Crashlytics receives a crash report with the device and system details needed to fix it. Mixpanel stores its data in the United States.

  • Not used for advertising. Analytics does not use Apple's advertising identifier, is not combined with data from other companies and is not used to show you ads or to track you.
  • Consent in the EEA, UK and Switzerland. There, usage analytics is switched on only if you allow it in the consent form described above, and you can withdraw that at any time in Durak's Settings under "Ad privacy choices". Crash reports are not linked to you and are sent regardless, so that crashes can be fixed.

What the app does not access

  • No location services
  • No camera, microphone or photo library
  • No contacts or calendar
  • No Health data
  • No account, email address or name

Children

Durak is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them.

Deleting your data

Deleting the app removes your progress, coins and settings from your device, and a new installation gets a new random identifier. To have purchase records removed from RevenueCat, or usage data removed from Firebase or Mixpanel, write to the address below and we will pass the request on. Information Google or Unity receive when showing ads is governed by Google's own policies and controls, which you can reach from the links above.
